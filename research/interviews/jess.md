**Date:** 9/21/2026


## Questions & Answers

Write what they said, as close to their own words as you can. Use quotes where possible.

**Q1: When was the last time you were late or barely on time to class or work?**
A: Yesterday, kind of. I had a 10:00 class and a 12:30 shift at the cafe. I made the class but I was literally running into the room. Then I was four minutes late to my shift because the train stopped for a few minutes.

**Q2: Tell me about that stretch of the day. What was going through your head?**
A: I was in class doing the math in my head the whole time. Like, if class ends at 11:50 and it takes 25 minutes to get there, I can still eat something. But then the professor kept talking until 12:05, and I was just watching the clock instead of listening.

**Q3: How do you keep track of your class schedule and your shifts?**
A: Classes are in my phone calendar. The cafe posts the schedule in a group chat every Sunday, and it changes every week. I screenshot it and put it as my lock screen. So I see it, but I don't really read it.

**Q4: What happens when a shift changes?**
A: My manager texts the group, "can anyone cover Thursday," and I might take it. Then I forget to put it anywhere except the chat. Twice I've shown up on the wrong day, which was embarrassing.

**Q5: Have you ever skipped something because you were running late for something else?**
A: Yes. I skipped lunch like three days a week. Or I leave class early, which I hate doing, but I have to. I'd rather be late to class than late to work because I get written up at work.

**Q6: What do you wish was different?**
A: I wish something just told me "you have to leave now" so I'm not doing math in class. I'd like to actually listen to the lecture.

## Synthesis

Fill this in right after the interview, while it's still fresh.

- **One surprising quote:** "I'd rather be late to class than late to work because I get written up at work."
- **One thing you didn't expect:** Jess is constantly managing lateness by trading things off: skipping lunch, leaving class early, picking which commitment to be late for. I thought lateness was about forgetting. For her it's about overbooked time and doing the math in her head all day.
- **What this makes you think about your problem:** Jess does the same mental countdown I do, and it costs her attention in the moment (she can't listen to the lecture). The most useful thing might not be a reminder at all, but removing the need to keep calculating. Also, her schedule lives in two places (calendar and a group chat), so a tool that only reads one source would miss half of it.
