Questions & Answers

Write what they said, as close to their own words as you can. Use quotes where possible.

Q1: When was the last time you were late or barely on time to a class? A: Tuesday. I teach at two different schools. My Tuesday class at the second school starts at 6:30, and I got there at 6:29. I had to set up the projector while students were already walking in.

Q2: Walk me through that afternoon. A: I had a morning class at the first school, then I went home, ate, and meant to rest for an hour. I lay down and fell asleep. I woke up at 5:15 and thought, "okay, I can still make it." The commute is 50 minutes. So it really wasn't fine.

Q3: What do you use to keep track of when and where you teach? A: I have a Google Calendar with all my classes. But it's four different colors and half of them are wrong because the schedule changes every semester and I forget to update it. I mostly trust my own head, and then check the calendar when I'm already unsure.

Q4: What happens when you check it and it's wrong? A: Honestly, that's the worst. Last semester the calendar said my class was at 5:30. It was at 5:00. I found out when a student texted me. So now I don't fully trust it, which means I double check with the department email, which takes even more time.

Q5: How do you feel right before a class you're running late for? A: Like a fraud. These are people paying to be there and I'm the one rushing in out of breath. I start the class apologizing, and it throws off the whole first ten minutes.

Q6: Is there anything you've tried that helped, even a little? A: I tried putting my train departure time in the calendar title, like "Leave 5:10." That worked for about two weeks. Then I stopped doing it because it's one more thing to type for every class.

Synthesis

Fill this in right after the interview, while it's still fresh.

One surprising quote: "I don't fully trust it, which means I double check with the department email, which takes even more time."
One thing you didn't expect: Marcus has a calendar and still doesn't trust it. I assumed the problem was lack of a reminder. His problem is that the source of truth is sometimes wrong, so he falls back on memory.
What this makes you think about your problem: A reminder tool is only useful if people believe the data underneath it. My idea might need to handle schedule changes automatically, or at least make it obvious when something looks outdated. His trick of writing the leave time in the title also suggests the useful number is "when to leave," not "when class starts."
