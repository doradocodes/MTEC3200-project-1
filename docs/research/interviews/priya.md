## Questions & Answers

**Q1: When was the last time you were late or barely on time to something you do regularly?**
A: Last Wednesday. I go to the office Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday and work from home Wednesday and Friday. I had an 8:30 team standup in person on a Wednesday that got moved from Zoom. I saw the invite the night before, I swear, but I still woke up and started my normal home routine. I got there at 9:05.

**Q2: Walk me through what happened that morning.**
A: Woke up at 7:30, which is my home wake up time. Made coffee, answered a few emails in my pajamas. Around 8:10 I looked at my calendar and saw "Standup, Conf Room B" and went, "oh no, that's in person."

**Q3: What's different about your office mornings versus your home mornings?**
A: Office days I have a whole system. Alarm at 6:45, bag packed the night before, I leave at 7:40 to catch the 7:52 train. Home days there's no structure. No train to catch, so nothing tells me when to be ready.

**Q4: Do you have anything that reminds you which kind of day it is?**
A: Not really. I know the pattern, but meetings get moved around, so the pattern breaks. My calendar has the info, but I don't look at it until I'm already in the morning.

**Q5: What do you do when you realize you're about to be late?**
A: Order a car. Which is about $30 I did not budget for. I've done it maybe five times this year. I keep telling myself I should just set another alarm.

**Q6: Why do you think you haven't?**
A: Because I feel like I shouldn't need one. I'm a responsible person. I'm just bad at the weird days, not the normal ones.

## Synthesis

Fill this in right after the interview, while it's still fresh.

- **One surprising quote:** "I'm just bad at the weird days, not the normal ones."
- **One thing you didn't expect:** Priya's lateness is almost entirely tied to days that break her pattern. On her normal office days she is fine. I assumed lateness was constant for people who struggle with it.
- **What this makes you think about your problem:** This is not about being generally unreliable. It's about exceptions: a moved meeting, a switched location, a different kind of day. A tool that only reminds people at the usual time would miss exactly the moments that matter. Also, she pays real money to fix it, which suggests the problem has actual cost.
